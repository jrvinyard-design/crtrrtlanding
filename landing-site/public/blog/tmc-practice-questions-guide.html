TMC Practice Questions: How Many You Actually Need (And Why Most Students Do It Wrong)

"How many practice questions should I do before the TMC?" is the wrong question. The right question is whether your practice actually matches how the real exam is weighted — and most static question banks don't.

The real TMC blueprint

The TMC has 140 scored items, split across three domains with very different weights. This is published directly by the NBRC, and it should be shaping how you spend your study time.

DomainItems% of Exam
I. Patient Data (evaluation, assessment, diagnostics)5036%
II. Troubleshooting, QC, Infection Control2014%
III. Initiation & Modification of Interventions7050%

Domain III alone is half the entire exam. If your practice questions are evenly split across topics — or worse, weighted toward whatever's easiest to write questions about — you're systematically under-practicing the single biggest category on test day.

It's not just topic, it's difficulty level too

The NBRC also classifies every item by cognitive level: Recall, Application, or Analysis. Across the full exam, the vast majority of items are Application or Analysis level — meaning you're synthesizing clinical data and deciding on an action, not just recalling a fact.

  • Only a small handful of items are pure Recall (simple fact lookup)
  • The bulk of the exam is Application and Analysis — "given this scenario, what do you do"

A lot of cheap question banks lean heavily on Recall-style trivia because it's easier and faster to write. That's a mismatch with what the actual exam demands.

Why static question banks run out of usefulness

Here's the practical problem with a fixed, unchanging bank of questions: once you've seen a question two or three times, you start recognizing the answer, not necessarily understanding the reasoning. You end up pattern-matching to a memorized key instead of practicing the actual clinical judgment the exam is testing.

That's fine for a first pass through content, but it stops being useful practice well before most students feel ready for the real exam.

What good TMC practice actually looks like

  • Weighted to match the blueprint — roughly half your practice time in Domain III, not spread evenly
  • Mostly Application/Analysis level — scenario-based questions with real clinical data, not flashcard-style recall
  • Fresh, not repeated — so you're being tested on reasoning every time, not memory of a specific question you've seen before
  • Explained thoroughly — not just "the answer is B," but why B is right and why A, C, and D are each wrong
